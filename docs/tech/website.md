# Website

Landing page that sells one promise: **we tell you which tasks a cheaper model is already good enough for.**

## Value prop (the sharp version)

**H1:** "Know which tasks a cheaper model is already good enough for."

**Sub:** "We replay your own traces through candidate models and grade the outputs — so you cut 30–50% of your LLM bill without quality risk. No fine-tuning, nothing in your live path."

## Page structure

1. **H1:** Know which tasks a cheaper model is already good enough for.
2. **Sub:** We shadow-evaluate your traces and hand you a per-task plan: which calls can move to a cheaper model, and what you'd save. If we're wrong, you've lost nothing.
3. **Primary CTA:** "Get my eval in 48 hours" (secondary: "Book a 15-min call")
4. **How it works (3 steps):**
   - Send us a week of traces (or drop in the 5-line wrapper).
   - We replay your tasks through candidate models and grade the outputs.
   - You get a routing plan: per-task "good enough" + projected savings.
5. **The fine-tuning flip (headline-worthy):**
   - "You may not need to fine-tune. We show you which repetitive tasks a cheaper model already handles."
6. **Trust section (answers the objection before it's asked):**
   - Your data → your model. Never pooled, never shared.
   - EU-resident storage.
   - No proxy in your request path. Logging is fire-and-forget; it can't break your calls.
   - Traces deletable on request.
7. **FAQ:** What do you do with my data? / Do you slow down my calls? / Which models do you support? / Is this fine-tuning? (No — it's evaluation.)

## Rules

- One promise, one CTA. Lead with the *eval* (the product), let the savings be the proof.
- "48 hours" and "30–50%" are specific and falsifiable — keep them.
- Speak to the *outcome* (know which task is safe to move), never the *mechanism* (observability).
- Never say "fine-tuning" as something we do — only as the thing you may not need.
